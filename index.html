§ 01 · Premise

From single-stage task solvers to long-running systems that reason, remember, and adapt.

Language agents are rapidly evolving from single-stage task solvers into long-running systems that reason, use tools, maintain memory, and adapt under changing tools, users, tasks, and safety constraints. Despite substantial progress in reasoning, tool use, alignment, and evaluation, the community still lacks a unified vocabulary for analyzing when agent capabilities should be invoked, constrained, updated, and evaluated over time.

This tutorial addresses that gap by organizing lifelong language agents around an agent lifecycle: foundations, capability formation, behavior and alignment, evolution, and evaluation. We introduce the Theory of Agent (ToA) as a diagnostic framework for analyzing knowledge boundaries, epistemic necessity, and effort allocation, and use it to connect methods for building, regulating, adapting, and evaluating agent systems.

Designed for students, researchers, and industry practitioners, the tutorial provides a compact field map, a reusable diagnostic vocabulary, a curated reading list, and a hands-on failure-diagnosis exercise.

The question that anchors everything: how should an agent decide when to reason internally, when to act externally, when to update, and how much effort to allocate over persistent interaction?

§ 02 · Outline

Six modules along the agent lifecycle.

From motivating failures to capability formation, alignment, evolution, and a hands-on failure-diagnosis exercise.

Module 01 · 10 min

Welcome & Motivating Failures

Representative failures that expose the limits of current language agents: outdated parametric knowledge, tool overuse, behavioral degradation under drift. From failure cases to the central question of the tutorial.

Module 02 · 30 min

Foundations of Agents

Theory of Agent (ToA) as a diagnostic framework. Three core concepts: knowledge boundary, epistemic necessity, and epistemic effort allocation. Why overthinking, underthinking, overacting, and underacting all share a single source.

Module 03 · 35 min

Capability Formation

How agents acquire capabilities for sustained operation: reasoning, planning, tool invocation and composition, memory, and interaction-based learning. Organized by the capabilities they add, not by individual systems.

Module 04 · 40 min

Behavior & Alignment

Think-versus-act routing, reasoning and tool budgets, memory-update decisions, clarification and stopping, tool overuse and underuse, and safety constraints. Alignment as a deployment-time control problem.

Module 05 · 30 min

Evolution of Agents

From single-session behavior to long-term evolution. Drift in tools, users, tasks, and safety. The movement of the knowledge boundary over time: internal capabilities, memory, learned skills, tool compositions, and world-model internalization.

Module 06 · 35 min

Evaluation & Failure Diagnosis

Why final-answer accuracy is insufficient. A multi-dimensional evaluation view: task success, efficiency, robustness, safety, memory quality, adaptation, and long-horizon stability. Closes with a hands-on failure-diagnosis exercise.

The question moves beyond whether an agent can reason or call tools in a single task. It becomes how an agent should allocate effort across internal reasoning, external action, memory use, updating, and stopping over time.

1.Foundations of Agents

This module introduces the diagnostic vocabulary of the tutorial — knowledge boundaries, epistemic necessity, and effort allocation. The readings span general reasoning-and-acting frameworks and the work that motivates the Theory of Agent perspective.

  • Position: Agents Should Invoke External Tools ONLY When Epistemically Necessary.Paper Hongru Wang et al.
  • ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models.Paper Shunyu Yao, Jeffrey Zhao, Dian Yu, Nan Du, Izhak Shafran, Karthik Narasimhan, Yuan Cao

Additional reading

  • Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models.Paper Jason Wei, Xuezhi Wang, Dale Schuurmans, Maarten Bosma, Brian Ichter, Fei Xia, Ed Chi, Quoc Le, Denny Zhou

3.Behavior & Alignment

Here we focus on deployment-time control: when an agent should reason, act, retrieve, or stop. The readings cover think-versus-act routing, tool overuse mitigation, retrieval gating, and the alignment paradigms (RLHF, DPO) that shape an agent’s behavior.
